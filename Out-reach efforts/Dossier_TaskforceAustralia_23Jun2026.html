The Wedge and the Matched Service
Taskforce manages property maintenance and safety compliance for housing providers and real estate agencies, deploying a 5,500-strong trade network through 19 in-house staff. Revenue reached $12.8 million in FY25 with 31 percent growth. The housing division is the acknowledged strongest and most profitable area, and housing job volumes grew 618 percent year-on-year, a rapid and significant shift in the business mix.

When one service line grows at that velocity, two questions become financially urgent. First, what margin does the housing business generate at scale once sub-contractor costs, scheduling technology, and coordination overhead are fully allocated? Second, as housing customers become an increasingly large share of job volume, what is the customer concentration profile and how exposed is the business to its largest housing accounts?

Neither question is answerable without a systematic mapping of service line margins and customer profitability. The Operational Intelligence Review runs that mapping across six weeks: customer concentration and profitability, product and service line margin, workforce capacity, and operational bottlenecks. The output is a prioritised action list for the 12 months ahead, calibrated to the equity plan and growth agenda the leadership team has already articulated.
