The Wedge and Matched Service

Taskforce Australia has built something genuinely unusual: a team of nineteen that manages $12.8 million in revenue through a national network of 5,500 tradespeople. The leverage ratio is exceptional. What the business now faces is not a capacity problem or a marketing problem. It is an architecture problem.

Three distinct revenue streams now run in parallel. The first is the compliance check model, transactional, driven by rental safety legislation, with a recurring client relationship but not recurring on individual jobs. The second is RentRepair, a subscription product launched in 2023 at $59 per month and above, which is structurally a recurring revenue business with a very different margin and churn profile. The third is HousingSolutions, contract based, government adjacent, and growing through preferred supplier relationships with community housing organisations.

Each stream demands a different financial management approach. Compliance services need client stickiness metrics and job profitability tracking. RentRepair needs subscriber acquisition cost, monthly recurring revenue, and churn visibility. HousingSolutions needs contract margin management and procurement efficiency. Running all three from the same team of nineteen, without a consolidated financial model that maps which stream generates the best return per hour of capacity, is the central risk of the next growth phase.

Added to this is the software investment decision. Taskforce has publicly stated plans to expand in house software capabilities to reduce delivery costs. That is a capital commitment with a compounding effect: commit too early and the business absorbs development cost before the revenue base can support it; commit too late and the competitive advantage is slower to materialise. The founders need a financial model that stress tests both paths and quantifies the cash flow and margin difference across scenarios before they commit.

The Strategic Growth Diagnostic is the right starting point. In six weeks it maps revenue headroom, capacity allocation, and margin across all three streams, then produces a twelve month growth plan with the capital allocation steps and three scenario modelling. It answers the sequencing question: where does the next dollar go and in what order.
