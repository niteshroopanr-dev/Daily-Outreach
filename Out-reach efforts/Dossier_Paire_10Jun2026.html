Phase D: Wedge and Matched Service

The Wedge

Paire has built something genuinely impressive: from zero to $10M in revenue in four years, Smart50 rank 15, Shark Tank Australia recognition, and a brand that 70 percent of customers return to. The growth record is verifiable across multiple independent publications.

The commercial challenge at this stage is not whether the business is growing. It is whether the growth is being generated equally across all the moving parts. Paire now operates four distinct commercial channels: DTC online, QV Melbourne CBD store, South Melbourne store, and wholesale export across 15 retail doors in Singapore and Malaysia. It sells across seven product categories: socks, underwear, bras, tops, leggings, activewear, and loungewear.

Each channel and each product category carries a materially different gross margin profile. DTC online: no intermediary, highest per unit margin. Physical retail: fixed lease, staff, and fit out costs that absorb contribution margin. Wholesale: lowest per unit margin but highest volume ceiling. Within products: premium natural fibre socks likely carry strong margin, but activewear and loungewear compete in a different segment with different cost structures.

CEO Nathan Yun has publicly stated the business is now prioritising profitability over hyper growth. That statement is an implicit acknowledgement that the current financial architecture does not clearly show which lines are winning. Without a product and channel profitability map, the natural tendency is to invest behind volume rather than margin. The Shark Tank capital of AUD 500,000 deserves to be directed at the highest return lines and channels, not the highest revenue ones.

The Product and Service Line Profitability engagement answers the question in three weeks: every product category and every revenue channel, ranked by gross margin, contribution margin, and operational drag, with a kill, fix, or scale decision on each. This is the one financial insight that would most directly inform the next 12 months of capital allocation, pricing decisions, and international expansion strategy.
