Commercial Wedge and Matched Service

Taskforce Australia has built a property maintenance technology platform that punches far above its weight. With 19 people generating $12.8 million in revenue, the business operates with exceptional efficiency. That is the product of a smart platform model: rather than employing tradespeople directly, Taskforce connects property managers and real estate agencies with a national network of over 5,000 tradespeople. The platform handles job allocation, scheduling, compliance tracking, and reporting. Three consecutive Smart50 listings with rank improving from 46 to 37 confirm this is a compound growth trajectory, not a single-year spike.

The founders have publicly stated a target of 50 to 60 percent compound annual growth over the next three years, funded organically. That ambition is credible given the platform economics. But executing it introduces a level of financial complexity that the current model has not yet had to navigate.

The complexity arrives from two directions simultaneously. First, RentRepair, launched at AREC 2023, is Australia's first subscription rental property maintenance product. It operates on materially different economics from the core compliance and job-allocation platform: subscribers are acquired upfront, revenue builds progressively over the subscription term, and the cash conversion timing is different from fee-per-job work. Second, growing the existing platform from $12.8 million toward $19 million or more in a single year through self-funded growth requires clear decisions about headcount sequencing, technology investment, and working capital.

The critical question for the founders is not whether to grow but how to allocate finite capital across two streams with different payback profiles, in what sequence, with what headcount plan, and what the cash impact looks like if subscriber acquisition is slower than forecast. Without a structured financial plan, the risk is either under-investing and losing momentum or deploying capital into one stream at the expense of the other.

The Strategic Growth Diagnostic is the right engagement here. Over six weeks, it maps revenue headroom, capacity at the team level, and margin by stream, then delivers a funded 12-month growth plan with base, downside, and upside scenarios. For a self-funded business at this scale with this ambition, that plan is the most leveraged financial document available.
