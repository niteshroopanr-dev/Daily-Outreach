5.3   The Wedge and Matched Service

Macro Mike is a food manufacturer at a significant inflection point. It has grown from a single SKU protein brownie in a one-bedroom apartment in 2016 to over 100 products in a 2,000sqm Gold Coast facility generating more than $10 million in annual revenue. Two brand collaborations in rapid succession, first the Golden Gaytime partnership in October 2024 (which drove December 2024 stock movement tripling year on year) and then the Kellogg's partnership launching to approximately 1,000 new store doors from November 2025, signal a business in a structural retail step-up.

The commercial challenge that follows this trajectory is predictable and well-documented in food manufacturing: cash is consumed by inventory before revenue is collected. A manufacturer fills warehouses, ships to retailers, and waits 30 to 60 or more days for payment. DTC channels are faster but carry their own inventory buffers. With 100+ SKUs across multiple channels, cash trapped in inventory, work in progress, and delayed receivables can represent 8 to 15 percent of annual revenue. At Macro Mike's current scale, that is between $800,000 and $1.5 million in cash that could be freed through structured working capital analysis and a resequenced collection and supplier terms model.

The Working Capital Unlock is a four-week ProfitPulse engagement that maps exactly where that cash is trapped, across debtors, inventory, work in progress, supplier terms and banking facilities, and produces a prioritised action list to release it. For a business about to push into approximately 1,000 additional store doors, this is not a nice-to-have. It is the difference between a growth move that fuels itself and one that quietly creates a liquidity crisis six months later.
